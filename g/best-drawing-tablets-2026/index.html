How to choose

  1. Pick the type first. Pen tablets are flat and screenless and the cheapest; pen displays let you draw on the screen but cost more; standalone tablets like the XPPen Magic Drawing Pad or an iPad need no computer (Creative Bloq).
  2. 8,192 pressure levels is plenty. Creative Bloq says to look for at least 8,192; TechRadar found it hard to tell 16K from 8K in use, and says higher can even be harder to control.
  3. Check what is in the box. Stands, shortcut remotes and cables are sometimes sold separately, which changes the real price (Creative Bloq, TechRadar).
  4. Size matters more than specs. For pen tablets Creative Bloq says Medium is the sweet spot; for displays it suggests 16 inches or larger for natural arm sweeps.

FAQ

Pen tablet or pen display?

A pen tablet is cheaper and easier on your posture but you look at your monitor while drawing. A pen display lets you draw right on the image, which many find more natural but costs more (Creative Bloq).

Is there a good alternative to the Intuos Pro?

Creative Bloq's best-value pen tablet is the Xencelabs Pen Tablet Medium Bundle at $329.99, which adds two pens and a Quick Keys remote.

Can I just use an iPad?

Yes. Creative Bloq's top standalone is the iPad Pro M5, but the Apple Pencil Pro is sold separately and the glossy screen feels slippier than a dedicated drawing tablet.
