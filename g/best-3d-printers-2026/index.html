How to choose

  1. Pick filament (FDM) first. Tom's Hardware says filament printers are easier to use and safer around kids and pets; resin gives finer detail but means toxic chemicals, a mask, and washing and curing every print.
  2. Treat auto bed leveling as a must. Tom's Hardware notes you can now find it under $250, so skip printers that make you level by hand.
  3. Match the machine to your filament. PLA prints on anything, PETG and TPU need hotends that reach about 220 to 230°C, and ABS or nylon need 240 to 250°C and ideally an enclosure (Tom's Hardware).
  4. Check the build volume. Tom's Hardware calls 250 mm cubed and above large format for desktop printers, and 150 to 220 mm average.

FAQ

Do I need an enclosed printer?

Only if you plan to print ABS, ASA or nylon. TechRadar notes that an open frame like the Kobra X limits material choice, while enclosed machines such as the X2D, Centauri Carbon and Core One handle tougher filaments.

Is multicolor printing worth it?

It is the most fun feature for beginners, but color changes create purge waste. Tom's Hardware measured the Kobra X at about 27% less waste than rivals, still more than a true tool changer.

Which is the cheapest good start?

The Bambu Lab A1 Mini, per TechRadar's budget pick, with the Anycubic Kobra X next if you want a bigger bed and faster color swaps.
