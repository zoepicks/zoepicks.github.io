How to choose

  1. Decide how hands-on you want to be. Manual machines like the Stilosa and Dedica Duo are cheapest but need skill. Guided machines such as the La Specialista Touch teach you as you go, and superautomatics do it all.
  2. Budget for a grinder. Tom's Guide calls the grinder the most important part of making espresso, so machines without one need a separate grinder, and a good one can cost $300 or more, though a hand grinder under $100 works for beginners.
  3. Ignore max-bar claims. Tom's Guide says the ideal pressure is 9 bar. A 15- or 20-bar rating just means headroom, not better coffee.
  4. Think about milk. If you make lattes, look for a strong steam wand or an automatic system. TechRadar found the Barista Touch Impress froths plant milk better than full-fat dairy.

FAQ

What is the best espresso machine for most people?

Tom's Guide picks the De'Longhi La Specialista Touch, while TechRadar names the Breville Barista Touch Impress with Cold Extraction its best overall.

Bambino or Bambino Plus?

Tom's Guide picks the cheaper Bambino and says the Plus adds automatic steaming but is slightly less good value. TechRadar names the Plus its best machine for small spaces.

Do I need a separate grinder?

Not with the La Specialista Touch, Barista Touch Impress, KitchenAid or Ninja Luxe Cafe, which have one built in. The Bambino, Stilosa and Dedica Duo do not.

What if I want to go deeper into espresso?

Tom's Guide recommends the Gaggia Classic E24 for enthusiasts. It is manual with a brass boiler and group head but no PID, so you manage the temperature yourself. For premium, it picks the Fellow Espresso Series 1.
