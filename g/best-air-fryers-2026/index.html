How to choose

  1. Compare cooking surface, not quarts. Tom's Guide says the quart rating is fairly meaningless; a wide, shallow basket that lets food lie flat gives better results than a tall narrow one.
  2. Check the wattage. More power means faster, crispier food, and Tom's Guide finds anything under about $50 tends to struggle.
  3. Ignore the presets. Preset modes only choose a time and temperature, so they make no difference to how the food cooks.
  4. Skip toaster-oven styles unless you need one. Tom's Guide finds them clunkier to clean and use than a basket air fryer.

FAQ

Are nonstick air fryers safe?

Tom's Guide says it is highly unlikely they are toxic and that PFAS does not automatically mean toxic. If you would still prefer to avoid nonstick, the COSORI Iconic is its PFAS-free pick.

Which air fryer is best for a family?

The Philips 3000 Dual Basket for cooking two foods at once, or the Ninja Pro XL if you prefer one large basket.

Is the Ninja Crispi a real air fryer?

Tom's Guide says it is not really one: it has four presets and no temperature control, and RTINGS found the Ninja Pro cooks better. It is best for small kitchens.
