5 mistakes that make a stand mixer a bad buy

  1. Buying the cheapest mixer. In CNN Underscored's test, the very small and very cheap models struggled with cookie dough and bread dough, and one performed like an undersized hand mixer.
  2. Ignoring weight. Heavier mixers stay put on the counter when the dough gets thick. If you bake bread or cookies often, look for a stronger motor (325 watts or more) and some heft.
  3. Not checking what is in the box. Most mixers ship with a paddle, a dough hook and a whisk, but some, like the Bosch Universal Plus, leave out cookie paddles you will want for cookie dough.
  4. Picking tilt-head when you make big bread batches. Bowl-lift and base-motor designs handle heavier dough with less rocking, and they leave the top open for adding ingredients.
  5. Skipping the warranty. The testers noted warranties from 1 year up to 7 years on the Ankarsrum motor. On a $400+ appliance, that is worth a look.

FAQ

What is the best stand mixer for most people?

The KitchenAid Artisan 5-Quart. It won all three of CNN Underscored's recipe tests and has the widest range of attachments.

Is the Cuisinart SM-50 good enough?

If you want to start baking without paying KitchenAid money, yes. It handled thick cookie dough and slack bread dough well in testing, but its whisk is awkward.

Which mixer is best for bread dough?

The Bosch Universal Plus and the Ankarsrum Original are the heavy-duty choices. Both have the motor in the base, which suits large batches and stiff dough.

Is the Ankarsrum worth the price?

Only if you bake bread several times a week or in large batches. CNN Underscored called it probably more machine than most people need.

Tilt-head or bowl-lift?

Tilt-head gives easier access to the bowl and beater. Bowl-lift and base-motor designs are steadier with heavy dough.
