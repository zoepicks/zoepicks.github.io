How to choose

  1. Match firmness to how you sleep. AARP suggests soft to medium-firm for side sleepers, medium to medium-firm for back sleepers, and medium-firm to firm for stomach sleepers.
  2. Always buy with a long trial. Tom's Guide says your body needs about 30 days to adjust, so get at least a 60-night trial; all of its top picks offer 100 nights or more.
  3. Hybrids bounce, foam isolates. Both outlets find pocketed-coil hybrids easier to move on, while memory foam is better at limiting motion between partners.
  4. Look at edges if you sit or share. Strong edges give you the full surface and a steady spot to push off from, which matters for couples and anyone with mobility issues (Tom's Guide).

FAQ

Hybrid or memory foam?

Hybrids add coils for bounce and airflow and are easier to move on; memory foam contours and isolates motion better but can trap heat (AARP).

What is AARP's top pick?

AARP ranked the Nectar Luxe Hybrid best overall, praising its motion isolation, pressure relief and cooling, though it noted below-average edge support.

What is the cheapest good option?

Tom's Guide's best cheap pick is the Siena Signature, a firm 10-inch foam bed that it says costs under $300 for a queen but is too stiff for most side sleepers.
